# Project brief: Pet Finder

## The competition
- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Middle school (grades 6–8)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: the registration deadline and the project submission deadline. For the registration deadline on December 18 you have to register before the 18th and for the project submission deadline on December 23rd you have to submit before that

## The problem
- people in my community struggle with loosing pets and not being able to find them because people see missing posters and never really pay attention to them.
- Impact area: Something else
- Why it matters (impact): Their lives will be better because it allows people to find their pets easier.
- Evidence it’s real: "Fifty-nine of the 156 (38%) pets were reunited with their owners" https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17571985/
- What people told me: My friend Ares said that he had lost his cat 5 different times and every time it took around and hour to days to find him.

## My solution
- My AI’s job: My AI helps by comparing missing pet posters and found animals. Which works better than missing posters because they don't draw much attention and if an animal is found and the posters are further away there will usually be no good way of telling who the animal belongs to.
- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Two tools are Petcolove.org and Pawboost.com which both help with missing animals but instead of comparing images and finding a match Its more based on human work to find the animals.
- Safety plan: scammers could attempt to contact pet owners under false pretenses and this would leave users vulnerable to dangerous situations. We could prevent this by requiring people to provide adequate evidence of the found animals. There will be warnings on the app telling people to be careful.

## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)
1. The user can compare missing pet posters with found animal images.
2. The user can submit evidence of their found pet for verification.
3. The user can report a found pet with details and images.
4. The user can receive warnings about potential scam behaviors.
5. The user can share their pet's poster on social media.
6. The AI gives correct, safe answers to real questions.

## Maybe later (not yet)
- The user can receive automated alerts about found pets.
- The user can view a gallery of found animals in their area.
- The user can receive notifications for matches between missing and found pets.
- The user can save their missing pet's profile for easy access.
- The user can search for missing pets by location and date.
- The user can read safety tips to avoid scams in the app.
- The user can contact local shelters for additional support.

## My AI’s instructions
- When it answers: The users will upload a photo of a found animal and the location and press enter.
- Its job: The AI will check photos of animals or missing posters to look for a match and alert the user of the found match.
- Who it talks to and how to sound: The AI will talk to users who have lost their pets or found a pet and it should sound professional and will answer in short clear sentences.
- Facts it must use: The AI will use facts and information from photos of missing posters and info provided by users.
- Rules it must follow: The AI should never definitively say that the pet is a 100 percent match and it shouldn't ask for personal information that could cause safety risks.
- How to answer: It should show the matches first from most likely to least likely to be the pet.
